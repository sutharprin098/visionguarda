Reviewer's opening note

Two figures in the supplied profile are mutually inconsistent: an overall pipeline latency of ~18 ms cannot coexist with a 25 ms single-pass inference stage if inference sits on the critical path. Section 1 addresses this before any recommendation is built on top of it. Every capacity number later in this document is derived from a stated model with visible assumptions, precisely so you can re-run it once the profile is corrected.

Executive SummaryThe Six Findings That Matter

The three objectives as posed each assume the answer is a technique — a faster transport, a smaller numeric format, a better scheduling algorithm. Two of the three turn out to be Phase 2 work. The highest-value changes available to this pipeline are a resize, a camera setting, and a scheduling policy, and together they fund a detector upgrade.

Table 0.1 — Headline findings, ordered by value per unit of effort
# Finding Effort Impact
1 Two supplied benchmark figures contradict each other. An 18 ms end-to-end latency cannot coexist with a 25 ms inference stage on the critical path. §1.1 1–2 days Resolving it swings 16-camera capacity between 2.5 and 15 FPS per camera — a factor of six. Nothing else should be committed until this is settled.
2 Your input geometry discards 43.75% of its own compute. Letterboxing 16:9 video into 416×416 leaves 182 of 416 rows as grey padding. 512×288 is exactly 16:9 and divisible by 32 on both axes. §3.3 Hours 15% fewer pixels and 1.23× higher linear content resolution simultaneously. Strictly better on both axes — the closest thing to free in the whole review.
3 Run analytics on camera substreams, not main streams. A 704×480 substream still exceeds your network input, so the detector loses nothing. §4.3 Hours Decode load falls to ~8% of current. This is a configuration change, and it is what makes the decode wall at 16 feeds disappear.
4 Stop inferring at 30–40 FPS. Association geometry puts the tracking floor near 6 FPS; static surveillance scenes are idle 80–95% of the time. Display at source rate with Kalman-interpolated overlays; analyse at 8 FPS, motion-gated. §4.4–4.5 3–5 days ~5× inference headroom. The largest algorithmic win available, and the enabler for finding 5.
5 Spend that headroom on model capacity, not frames. YOLOX-S at 512×288 costs 1.75× YOLOX-Tiny and is infeasible at uniform 8 FPS — but comfortable under motion gating, at 33% utilisation. §5 1–2 weeks +7.7 COCO AP, with a wider gap on small objects — which is exactly the night-time regime this system exists to serve. This is the strategic recommendation.
6 Objective 1 is narrower than it looks. Of three ingest surfaces, only Windows Graphics Capture genuinely needs Electron→Python bulk pixel transfer; RTSP and HLS should be decoded in Python directly. The return path wants hardware H.264 plus WebCodecs, not shared memory. §2.1, §2.7 2–4 weeks The shared-memory ring is still worth building for WGC (7–13 ms plus GC pressure removed), but the scope is one path in three. The D3D11 zero-copy extension is probably not worth building at all — the tensor is 216 KB. A.2
How to read this document

Sections 2–4 answer the three stated objectives in order. Section 1 audits the supplied profile first, because several later recommendations invert depending on how the benchmark contradiction resolves. Section 5 is the strategic argument, Section 6 is the sequenced backlog, and Appendix C states the confidence level of every load-bearing claim along with how to falsify it — this review was produced without access to the code, the footage, or the hardware, and it says so where that matters.
